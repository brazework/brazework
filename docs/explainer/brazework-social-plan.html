Launch day

  1. 12:01 am Pacific: the page goes live. Post the maker's first comment straight away.
  2. Morning: the launch video on X, LinkedIn, Shorts and Reels, each linking the Product Hunt page.
  3. All day: both founders answer every comment on Product Hunt and every platform. Ask people to look, never to upvote.

House rules

  • Real people only. No made-up users, testimonials or numbers. Anything from the demo stack is labelled "Example" on screen.
  • Film people only with their permission, including everyone at the Rocket Showcase and in the pilot.
  • Never show or mention anything from Josh's or Ian's employers.
  • Every video has burned-in captions. Most people watch on mute, and it's the accessible way.
  • Post from Josh's and Ian's own accounts first, and share from the Brazework accounts. People follow people.
  • Every link carries its source: brazework.com/?ref=x-w1 (platform, then week).
  • One call to action per post, and it's always something a viewer can do alone: "Log your first build in 60 seconds."
  • Reply to every comment within a few hours. Early replies matter more than the post itself.

Decision rules

  • After week 3, double down on the platform where the most people logged a first build, not the one with the most views.
  • A platform with views but no first builds gets one change of hook, then gets dropped to sharing only.
  • If sign-ups come in but few log a build, the problem is the first five minutes, not the posts. Look at /start and onboarding.
  • If builds are logged but few are signed, film more signer moments (week 3) and check that signature emails go out.

What this plan depends on

  • Build-first onboarding (#100) and /start live by week 2. Until then, posts point to /setup.
  • Signature emails actually sending in production.
  • Sign-ups recording their ?ref source.
  • The TEST milestone hidden or revoked before Nov 22.
  • Permission from everyone who appears on camera.

After launch: the outreach agent

Once there's signed work to point to, an agent researches each contact and drafts a personal email that cites real Brazework work. A founder reviews and sends every one. Start with the Connecticut employers in the strategy doc (a paid pilot sponsoring one chapter), then universities once a pilot club has a semester of signed work. Keep the volume low and the emails personal: signature emails share the same sending domain, so its reputation matters.
