Your AI drafts the proof. A person who saw it signs.

Brazework is the record of what you build, signed by the people who saw it. With its MCP server, the assistant you already use (Claude, Cursor, VS Code) reads your work where it lives, in GitHub, Onshape, Weights & Biases, Figma or Stripe, and drafts the milestone with the evidence attached. You approve it. A club officer, professor or manager signs it.

AI can write a résumé in seconds. It can't produce a professor's signature on a flight log. That gap is the business.

How the whole thing works

Brazework doesn't build sixty integrations. Each tool vendor (GitHub, Autodesk, MathWorks, Figma, Stripe) maintains its own MCP server, and the assistant connects them. Brazework adds one server at /api/mcp, and the assistant chains them: it reads the work from one server and writes the proof to the other.

  1. Your tools

    GitHub, Onshape, KiCad, W&B, MATLAB, ANSYS, Overleaf, Benchling, Figma, Stripe, Qualtrics. Each field has a playbook that says how the assistant reaches each one.

  2. Your assistant

    Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Cursor, VS Code, Codex, Windsurf, Zed. One command connects Brazework with your token.

  3. Brazework MCP

    5 slash commands (shipped, result, week, ask_signature, setup), 12 tools, and a playbook resource for every field.

  4. You approve

    Yes in chat, or tap Post on the draft in /drafts from your phone. Nothing goes out without you.

  5. Someone who saw it signs

    A club officer verifies it, or a professor, PI or manager signs through a one-time email link. They don't need an account.

  6. Companies find you

    Find builders searches by verified work. Every result shows who signed and when.

How far it goes

What's live, what's designed next, and the bigger bets. Four lines never move: the AI never verifies, never signs, never changes or deletes what you made, and never posts without your yes. Those rules are why a signature means something.

Live today shipped

  • /shipped: finished work becomes a milestone with 1 to 8 evidence links.
  • /result: a run, test or sales total becomes a result post with the numbers.
  • /week: 7 days across every connected tool, 1 to 3 drafts, no repeats.
  • /ask_signature: emails a professor or manager a 14-day, single-use link (school or company emails only, never a teammate, 3 per milestone, 10 a week).
  • GitHub Action: every merged PR leaves a draft milestone with the PR as evidence.
  • Officers read their verify queue from chat (my_review_requests), then verify in Brazework.
  • Drafts inbox, with "N drafts from your tools" on Today.

Designed next not built

  • Officer weekly digest through MCP: who's waiting, what the club shipped, what to tell sponsors.
  • Uploading evidence files (photos, PDFs, logs) straight from the assistant, not just links.
  • Scheduled automations beyond GitHub: a nightly W&B check, or a scheduled Claude task that runs /week every Friday.

Bigger bets ideas

  • Recruiter MCP: a company's own AI agent searches verified work ("load cell, signed by a professor, 2027 grads"). It's a paid seat and the strategy doc already calls for it.
  • Lab and team plan: a whole lab or company team on GitHub Actions and drafts, with the PI or manager signing in one batch.
  • Listing in MCP directories (Claude's connector directory and MCP registries) so builders find Brazework where they already add tools.

Why the MCP sells Plus

The free tier gets builders hooked on drafts that write themselves. Unlimited drafts, private builds and the PDF portfolio are what a serious builder upgrades for, right when /week starts finding three things a week.
